Setup guide

Welcome — and thank you for helping test caidal. This takes about 10 minutes, and no tech skills are needed. If you can install an app, you can do this.

You'll need an iPhone (or iPad) on a recent version of iOS. If anything won't install, it's usually because the phone needs a quick update: Settings → General → Software Update.

1 · Get the app (three steps)

caidal isn't in the App Store yet, so we use Apple's official tool for trying apps early, called TestFlight. It's made by Apple and completely safe.

  • Install TestFlight — open the App Store, search “TestFlight,” and tap Get (it's free).
  • Accept your invitation — open the “You're invited to test caidal” email on your iPhone and tap “View in TestFlight,” then Accept.
  • Install caidal — tap Install. That's it — caidal is now on your home screen.

Because it's a test version, caidal may update often. When it does, TestFlight will let you know — just tap Update.

2 · Your first time opening caidal

  • A quick welcome tour. The first launch opens with a short intro — what caidal is, how it works, and how it treats your data. It's a few taps, and you can hit “Skip” (top-right) anytime.
  • One thing to accept. On the privacy screen you'll tick a short tester consent — a plain-language “this is an early beta, not medical advice” note. It needs to be checked to continue.
  • Optional boosts. On the final “You're ready” page you can pull your Apple Health records, add a basic profile, and pick your assistant's model. All optional — a green or orange check shows what you've done.
  • Straight into your first visit. Tap “Start my first visit” and caidal drops you right into a new visit to try things out. Prefer to look around first? Choose “Skip for now.”
  • For this beta the welcome tour shows on every launch so you can re-test it — just tap Skip to breeze past.

3 · Apple Health (totally optional)

caidal can connect to Apple Health to read your clinical records so its answers are more tailored to you. This is optional — the app works fully without it. If you'd rather not, just skip that step.

  • If you'd like to try it, tap “Turn On All” (or pick items) when caidal asks, then Allow.
  • caidal only reads what you approve; you can turn it off anytime in Settings → Privacy & Security → Health.
  • On an iPad, Apple Health isn't available — that's normal; everything else still works.

4 · Is your information safe?

  • Sharing is your choice, item by item — by default your data stays on your phone.
  • What you do share is sent securely to OpenAI only to generate your answers.
  • Your data is not used to train AI models, and not visible to anyone else.
  • You're always in control — review, remove, or delete anything at any time.

5 · How to share your thoughts

This is the whole point — your honest first impressions. Two easy ways:

  • Inside TestFlight: take a screenshot while using caidal, and TestFlight will offer to send it to us with a note.
  • Or just email us anything — what confused you, what you liked, what felt off. Even “I didn't get it” is incredibly useful.

That's everything. Thank you for being early — the first people to believe in something are the ones who make it real. 🙌

Step-by-step testing guide

We're building something we genuinely care about, and we want you in early — before it's finished, while your reaction can still shape it.

Intro

caidal is an app to help people get more out of their doctor visits: walk in prepared, understand what actually happened, leave with a clear plan, and carry the whole story into the next visit. Most of us leave appointments foggy, forget half of what was said, and start from zero every time. caidal is our attempt to fix that.

Here's why we're asking you specifically: the first handful of people to try something are the ones who shape what it becomes. Your confusion, your “wait, what does this do?”, even your “I'd never use this” — that's the most valuable feedback we can get while it's still soft clay. It's ~15–20 minutes, you can't break anything, and “I didn't get it” is genuinely useful. Thank you — it means a lot. 🙏

caidal's model

caidal has a simple model, and it's all about one thing: supporting your visit experience so it's done thoroughly and reliably. A few ideas make that work.

  • Visits are organized by problem. caidal groups your visits under the problem they address, so it can follow the whole journey — and each visit is aware of the ones before it. Continuity of care, built in.
  • Your health data lives in four types — clinical records, personal data, documents, and notes. Attach any of them to as many related visits as you like, to give caidal evidence and sharpen the answers it produces for that visit.
  • Visits produce episodes and documents — recordings you make and files you add. From these, caidal extracts alerts (things worth paying attention to), findings (what was learned about you), and actions (steps to take for your health). Crucially, every extraction is grounded in the wider context — the visit itself and all of your data — not just the single recording or page.

caidal's heartbeat

Two simple rhythms run through the whole app. Feel them once and everything clicks.

Generate → Develop → Commit. Almost everything caidal makes for you follows this beat. You kick off a generate right where it belongs — ask it to prepare you for a visit from the chat, or pull alerts, results, and next steps from your recordings and documents inside the visit. From there you develop the draft in the chat — refine, add, reshape — with the follow-up actions appearing as buttons just above the message box, then you commit, and it lands in its hub entry, ready to use.

The visit hub is home base for a single visit: Preparation, Visit, Alerts, Results, Next steps — each one both shows what you've committed and is where you generate it. Rule of thumb: right after you commit, open the matching hub entry to see what you locked in.

Capture → Refine → Save. The second rhythm is getting your health story in with zero friction, through Scribbles. The moment something comes to mind — a symptom, a worry, a measurement, a question — jot or speak a quick Scribble. Don't organize it, don't make it perfect; just get it out of your head while it's fresh. Later you shape it into a Note or a Personal Data point — or even an Alert, a Result, or a Next step — and attach it to any visit. The more complete your story, the more personal and accurate caidal becomes.

Optional first move (one tap)

caidal can pull your real medical records straight from Apple Health with a single button — instant, realistic data to explore with. Totally optional, though: if you'd rather not, or you don't have any records in Apple Health, that's completely fine — nothing here is required, and skipping it won't break or block anything.

And if you do pull them: your records stay on your device, and caidal only reads what you approve (read-only). Nothing is ever sent anywhere unless you personally choose to share a specific item with the assistant.

Before you dive in

The app comes preloaded with example visits and data, some of which you'll use at the end of the walkthrough. For now, stick to the walkthrough script and explore more later.

The tasks for you to try (≈15–20 min)

Pick a “case.” Think of a health thing you've dealt with — a trick knee, migraines, blood pressure, a recent check-up. Nothing comes to mind? Make one up (say, recurring headaches). That's your storyline.

1 · Start a visit — your way

  • Create a new visit for your case (e.g. “Headaches — primary care”). It opens straight into a chat with a short intake form.
  • Try both paths (the big new thing we'd love your read on): either fill it in — only Title and Chief complaint are required — and tap Submit; or tap Skip form and just talk, describing what's bothering you and what you want out of the visit in your own words.
  • Either way, the message box opens and you're chatting — tell caidal what's on your mind and ask whatever you want.

2 · Get prepared (the heartbeat)

  • Whenever you feel ready — right away, or after a few back-and-forth messages — tap the ⋯ menu (top-right) → “Prepare me for the visit”. That's the one command in that menu; caidal drafts your prep from the conversation so far.
  • Develop the draft in the chat — refine it, add a question, reshape it until it feels like your briefing (the follow-up actions show up as buttons just above the message box) → then commit. Open the Preparation hub entry to see what you locked in. (The pattern everywhere: once you commit, check the matching hub entry.)

3 · Give it context

  • Attach related data to the visit via ⋯ menu → Data → Add — a personal data point (a measurement, a symptom) or a medical record (if you pulled from Apple Health). Old or rough is fine; we just want the flow.

4 · “Attend” the visit

  • Open the Visit hub entry and record an episode — hit record and talk for a bit, saying some doctor-visit-sounding things out loud. Stop, and check the transcription. (Record as many episodes as you like.)
  • Right there on the episode, pull out what was said: generate its Alerts (things worth a closer look), Results (findings), and Next steps (actions) → develop → commit. Each lands in its matching hub entry.

5 · Pull insights from a document

  • Add a document inside the visit: ⋯ menu → Data → Add → Document — a lab or test result, a post-visit summary, or a discharge note (an old real one is perfect).
  • Then extract from it inside the matching hub entry: open Alerts to pull its alerts, Results to pull its findings, and Next steps to pull its actions — each via generate → develop → commit. A great way to make sense of dense labs, test results, and after-visit paperwork.

6 · Try Scribbles (quick capture)

  • Create a text scribble about your case → convert it into a Note.
  • Create an audio scribble — just speak out a health observation (e.g. “Slept badly again, morning headache about a 6 out of 10”) → convert it into Personal Data.
  • Add both to your visit.

7 · See the journey (continuity of care)

  • From the Problems tab, open Ankle sprain — four example visits for the same problem, laid out on the journey map: each visit a stop along the path, the newest ringed. Tap between them, toggle map / list, and check the legend (“what the dots mean”). (You can also reach it from inside any visit via ⋯ menu → “Show full visits trail.”)
  • Open the latest and notice it already knows about the earlier ones. That “it remembers, so you don't have to” thread is the heart of the whole app.

Do as much or as little as you like — even steps 1–2 are a huge help. And once you've finished, feel free to roam: explore the example visits, dig through the ⋯ menu, break things. When you're done, screenshot anytime inside TestFlight (it'll offer to send it to us with a note) or just text/email us: what confused you, what you liked, what felt slow or pointless. Brutal honesty is a gift.
